Friction and Braking Distance

A child steps onto a zebra crossing ahead. Can the car stop in time? Friction between the tyres and the road decides.

Ready

Stopping distance grows faster than speed

Reaction distance grows in step with speed; braking distance grows with speed squared. Double the speed and the braking distance becomes four times as long.

Key terms

Friction and braking

When the driver brakes, friction between the tyres and the road is the force that slows the car. On level road it gives a deceleration of about a = μg.

Reaction distance

Distance travelled while the driver notices the danger and moves to the brake: speed × reaction time. The car is still at full speed.

Braking distance

Distance travelled after the brakes are applied: v² / (2μg). It depends on speed squared and on the road's friction.

Stopping distance

Reaction distance + braking distance.

Why tyres have treads

Treads push water away so rubber stays in contact with the road, keeping friction high on wet roads.

A simplified model for learning: level road, constant friction while braking, typical coefficient values and g = 9.8 m/s². Real stopping distances also depend on tyres, brakes and load. Not a driving guide.
