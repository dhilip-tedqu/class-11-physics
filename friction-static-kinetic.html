While the box is at rest, friction equals the pull. At limiting friction the box starts to move, and friction drops to the smaller kinetic value. Your own readings appear as dots.

Try this

  1. Press Increase the pull slowly. At what force does the box start to move? Compare it with μsN.
  2. Once it moves, reduce the pull to just above μkN. Why does the box keep moving with a smaller force?
  3. Double the mass. What happens to the force needed to start it?
  4. Put wheels under the box. How much smaller is the force now? This is rolling friction.

Key terms

Static friction

Friction on an object at rest. It matches the applied force, up to a limit.

Limiting friction

The largest static friction, fmax = μsN. A bigger pull starts the motion.

Kinetic (sliding) friction

Friction on a sliding object, fk = μkN. It is less than limiting friction.

Rolling friction

Friction when an object rolls instead of slides. It is much smaller, which is why wheels and ball bearings make work easier.

Normal force

The upward push of the floor on the box. On level ground, N = mg.
