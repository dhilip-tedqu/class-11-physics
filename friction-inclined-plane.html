Friction as the plane tilts

Static friction rises with the angle until it reaches its limit at θr. Then the block slides and friction drops to kinetic friction, μkN.

Key terms

Friction

A force that opposes sliding between two surfaces in contact. It acts along the surface.

Normal force (N)

The push of the surface on the block, at right angles to the surface. On an incline, N = mg cos θ, so it gets smaller as the plane gets steeper.

Static friction

Friction while the block is at rest. It adjusts itself to exactly balance the pull along the slope, up to a maximum.

Limiting friction

The maximum static friction: fmax = μsN. When the pull along the slope exceeds this, the block starts to slide.

Kinetic friction

Friction while the block is sliding: fk = μkN. It is usually less than limiting friction, so it is easier to keep something moving than to start it.

Coefficient of friction (μ)

A number that tells how rough a pair of surfaces is. It has no unit. Higher μ means more grip.

Angle of repose (θr)

The largest angle at which the block stays at rest on the incline; also called the limiting angle. At this angle tan θr = μs. It does not depend on mass.

Angle of friction (λ)

The angle between the normal force and the resultant of normal force and limiting friction: tan λ = μs. So the angle of friction equals the angle of repose.

This is a simplified model for learning: it assumes a rigid block, no air resistance, constant coefficients and g = 9.8 m/s². Coefficients for the surfaces are typical textbook values; real values vary with condition and cleanliness.
